A car engine cylinder contains several key components that work together to convert fuel into motion:

The **piston** moves up and down inside the cylinder bore, connected to the **crankshaft** by a **connecting rod**. At the top, the **cylinder head** seals the chamber and houses the **intake and exhaust valves** that control air/fuel flow and exhaust gases. 

The **spark plug** (in gasoline engines) ignites the compressed air-fuel mixture. **Piston rings** seal the piston against the cylinder wall to contain combustion pressure and control oil. The space above the piston when it's at the top forms the **combustion chamber** where the explosion happens.

During operation, the cylinder goes through four strokes: intake (fuel/air in), compression (piston squeezes mixture), power (spark ignites, explosion pushes piston down), and exhaust (burned gases out).
